This article won't pretend everything is fine. Instead, it will explain:
- What actually happened with XOR supply
- Why the team made these decisions
- What the Token Bonding Curve is and how it works
- What changed with the 2025 repackaging events
- Whether the tokenomics have genuinely improved
- What realistic scenarios look like going forward

If you want honest answers, keep reading.

### The Result

More XOR in circulation, with demand not keeping pace, meant the price per token fell. This is basic economics: if you increase supply faster than demand, price drops.

<div class="not-prose my-6 rounded-lg border-l-4 border-amber-500 bg-amber-500/10 p-4">

## The Token Bonding Curve: SORA's Core Mechanism

XOR doesn't have a fixed supply like Bitcoin. Instead, it has an **elastic supply** managed by a smart contract called the Token Bonding Curve (TBC).

### How It Works

The TBC is essentially a decentralized market maker with two functions:

<div class="not-prose my-6 overflow-x-auto">
  <table class="w-full text-left text-sm">
    <thead class="border-b border-gray-700 text-xs uppercase text-gray-400">
      <tr>
        <th class="px-4 py-3">Function</th>
        <th class="px-4 py-3">What Happens</th>
        <th class="px-4 py-3">Effect on Supply</th>
      </tr>
    </thead>
    <tbody class="divide-y divide-gray-800">
      <tr>
        <td class="px-4 py-3 font-semibold text-green-400">Buy from TBC</td>
        <td class="px-4 py-3">Pay reserve assets (DAI, ETH, etc.) → Receive newly minted XOR</td>
        <td class="px-4 py-3">Supply increases</td>
      </tr>
      <tr>
        <td class="px-4 py-3 font-semibold text-red-400">Sell to TBC</td>
        <td class="px-4 py-3">Send XOR → Receive reserve assets, XOR is burned</td>
        <td class="px-4 py-3">Supply decreases</td>
      </tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>
</div>

The buy price is always ~20% higher than the sell price (per current parameters — this can be adjusted by governance). This spread:
- Builds reserves over time
- Creates arbitrage bounds (limiting extreme volatility)
- Funds KUSD stabilization operations (per current parameters)

### The Ideal vs. Actual Scenario

At launch, the TBC had an "unfunded liability" — XOR existed before reserves were built. This meant:
- The **ideal sell price** (20% below buy) wasn't achievable
- The **actual sell price** was lower, based on available reserves
- As reserves build, actual approaches ideal

<div class="not-prose my-6 rounded-lg border-l-4 border-blue-500 bg-blue-500/10 p-4">
  <p class="text-sm"><strong>Key insight:</strong> The TBC doesn't guarantee price stability — it provides "forward guidance" on price ranges. When the secondary market (Polkaswap, CEXs) trades outside TBC bounds, arbitrage opportunities pull price back. But if no one arbitrages, the bounds don't enforce themselves.</p>
</div>

For a deeper dive, see the <a href="https://wiki.sora.org/tbc.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">SORA Wiki on Token Bonding Curve</a>.

## The 2025 Repackaging: What Changed (And What Didn't)

In 2025, SORA underwent two **denomination repackaging** events. These are often misunderstood.

### May 2025: 100:1 Repackaging

Every 100 XOR on the SORA network became 1 XOR. If you held 1,000 XOR, you now held 10 XOR.

### October 2025: 1:1,000,000 Repackaging

A further adjustment grouped 1,000,000 old units into 1 new unit.

## What's Actually Changed in Tokenomics

Beyond cosmetic repackaging, there have been substantive changes to how XOR supply is managed:

### 1. TBCD Funding Continues (But It's Transparent)

TBCD (Token Bonding Curve Dollar) is a mechanism where governance mints stablecoins that buy XOR from the TBC, expanding supply. This is how SORA funds development — and it's still active.

Every month, SORA's governance process approves invoices paid in TBCD. These are published in the <a href="https://github.com/orgs/sora-xor/projects/24" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">monthly memos on GitHub</a>, so you can see exactly what's being funded and how much TBCD is minted. (The full Parliament model with multi-body sortition is planned for SORA v3/Nexus; current governance uses Council and Technical Committee structures.) This transparency is unusual in crypto.

**The trade-off:** TBCD minting expands XOR supply, which dilutes existing holders. The argument is that this funds productive development that should eventually increase demand. Whether that trade-off is worth it depends on whether the development delivers value.

### 2. TONSWAP Fee Burns

When [TONSWAP](/introducing-tonswap-the-dex-for-mass-adoption-on-ton) launches fully, 10% of trading fees will be allocated to **buy back and burn XOR**. This creates a direct link between TON ecosystem trading activity and XOR deflation.

The more volume TONSWAP generates, the more XOR gets burned. This is a real deflationary mechanism tied to actual usage, not just tokenomics promises.

### 3. Transaction Fee Burns

Every SORA v2 transaction burns a small amount of XOR as fees. With enough network activity, burns can exceed emissions, making supply deflationary.

### 4. Kensetsu XOR (Future)

SORA is developing "Kensetsu XOR" — a proposed overcollateralized synthetic designed to track XOR's price. The idea: fund productive economy expansion without diluting XOR supply. Still in development; mechanics may evolve.

<div class="not-prose my-6 rounded-lg border-l-4 border-green-500 bg-green-500/10 p-4">
  <p class="text-sm"><strong>Net effect:</strong> TBCD still funds development (inflationary), but burn mechanisms now exist (deflationary). Whether supply grows or shrinks depends on the balance between development spending and network activity generating burns.</p>
</div>

## Realistic Scenarios: What Could Happen

Let's be honest about what would need to happen for XOR's situation to improve.

### Scenario 1: Sustained High Network Activity

If Polkaswap volume, [TONSWAP](/introducing-tonswap-the-dex-for-mass-adoption-on-ton) launch, and CBDC integrations drive significant transaction volume, fee burns could exceed emissions. Supply would shrink.

**Likelihood:** Moderate. Requires sustained adoption, not just announcements.

### Scenario 2: Institutional Adoption via Hub Chain

If countries like those testing CBDCs (Cambodia, PNG, Solomon Islands) integrate with SORA's Hub Chain and create demand for XOR as a settlement layer, this could drive legitimate buy pressure. For more on the Hub Chain, see [SORA Nexus Complete Guide](/sora-nexus-complete-guide).

**Likelihood:** Low-to-moderate. Pilots exist, but full integration is years away.

### Scenario 3: Governance-Mandated Burns

The community could vote to burn treasury XOR or implement more aggressive burn mechanisms.

**Likelihood:** Possible. Depends on community consensus and whether treasury funds are needed for development.

### Scenario 4: Status Quo

Network activity remains modest. Burns roughly match emissions. Supply stays flat. Price depends on broader crypto market sentiment.

**Likelihood:** Most likely in the short term.

<div class="not-prose my-6 rounded-lg border-l-4 border-blue-500 bg-blue-500/10 p-4">
  <p class="text-sm"><strong>Time horizons:</strong> Most of these catalysts — TONSWAP volume, CBDC integration, Hub Chain adoption — are multi-quarter or multi-year developments. Don't expect overnight changes.</p>
</div>

<div class="not-prose my-6 rounded-lg border-l-4 border-amber-500 bg-amber-500/10 p-4">
  <p class="text-sm"><strong>Bottom line:</strong> There's no magic fix. Recovery requires real utility — transactions, swaps, settlements — not just tokenomics changes. The infrastructure is being built. Whether it gets used is the question.</p>

## Conclusion: Eyes Open

XOR's supply expanded massively. This diluted early holders. That's the reality.

The team's bet was that building infrastructure would eventually create enough utility to justify the supply. Whether that bet pays off depends on:

1. **SORA v3 (Nexus) delivering** on its Hub Chain promise
2. **Real adoption** — CBDCs, TONSWAP, Polkaswap volume
3. **Community governance** making smart allocation decisions

If you're holding XOR, you're betting on long-term utility, not short-term tokenomics. Go in with eyes open.

## FAQs

<details>
<summary>Did the repackaging make me richer?</summary>

No. Repackaging is cosmetic — it changes display units, not value. If you owned 0.001% of XOR supply before, you still own 0.001% after. The price per token is higher because there are fewer tokens, but total value is unchanged. Think of it like exchanging 100 pennies for 1 dollar.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Why did XOR inflate so much?</summary>

To fund development. Building a blockchain ecosystem — wallets, DEXs, bridges, CBDC infrastructure — costs money. The team chose to fund this via XOR emission rather than VC funding or ICOs. The trade-off was dilution of existing holders.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Is XOR supply still inflating?</summary>

Yes, but with more transparency. TBCD is still minted to fund development (you can see exactly how much in the monthly Community Memos). Validator rewards also emit XOR. Transaction fee burns offset some of this, and TONSWAP's 10% fee burn will add deflationary pressure once it's live. Net effect depends on network activity vs. development spending.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Will there be a big burn to fix the supply?</summary>

Maybe, but don't count on it. Governance could vote to burn treasury XOR, but those funds may be needed for development. The main deflationary pressure comes from transaction fee burns and TONSWAP's 10% fee burn mechanism — both tied to actual network usage.
</details>

<details>
<summary>What's the Token Bonding Curve and does it help?</summary>

The TBC is a smart contract that mints XOR when you buy (with reserve assets) and burns XOR when you sell. It creates price bounds via arbitrage. It doesn't guarantee price stability, but it does provide some predictability. The catch: it only works if people actually arbitrage. See the <a href="https://wiki.sora.org/tbc.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">TBC documentation</a> for details.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Should I buy XOR?</summary>

This article doesn't give financial advice. If you're considering XOR, understand that you're betting on long-term utility (CBDCs, TONSWAP, Polkaswap adoption), not short-term tokenomics. The infrastructure is real, but adoption is uncertain. DYOR.
</details>

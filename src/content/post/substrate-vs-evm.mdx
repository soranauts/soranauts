Blockchain builders have a big choice to make: use Substrate or EVM for building on Polkadot and Ethereum. These are tools that help people create blockchains and manage how they work.

Polkadot lets lots of different blockchains talk to each other using something called [parachains](https://www.soranauts.com/polkadot-parachain-advantages), while Ethereum is like one big computer that everyone shares.

Substrate is like a box of Lego pieces that you can put together however you want to build your own custom blockchain. It's flexible and supports many ways to keep your blockchain safe.

On the other hand, EVM stands for Ethereum Virtual Machine, which is more like a standard engine used by everyone in the Ethereum world.

People who build with Substrate can make their own new chains or connect them as part of Polkadot or Kusama's network. This gives them lots of options to choose from. The smart contracts – these are special programs on the blockchain – work differently too! With [Substrate](https://substrate.io/vision/substrate-and-polkadot/), you can run smart contracts just like on Ethereum but with extra features and having control over transaction costs.

SORA chose to go with Polkadot and Substrate because it fits their project called Polkaswap really well. They looked at everything carefully before deciding!

Now imagine all the cool things we could do if different blockchains could share information easily! That's what bridges between networks like Polkadot and Ethereum aim to do someday soon.

This article will tell you about these tools so you can see why they are important for making blockchains better. Let's unwrap this tech puzzle together!

### Key Takeaways

- Substrate and EVM are different tools for creating blockchains. Substrate is flexible, letting you make custom chains that work with Polkadot. EVM is set up to work with Ethereum's network.
- Polkaswap uses Substrate because it can change to fit their needs and also works well with Ethereum projects.
- Scalability is a big challenge for blockchains. Polkadot was made to scale better by having many chains run at the same time. This keeps it fast even when lots of people use it.
- Smart contracts are important in blockchain. They need careful coding and testing before being used. With Frontier EVM, developers can move their projects from Ethereum to a Substrate-based chain easily.
- Gas fees are like prices for doing things on the blockchain. Both Polkadot and Ethereum have these fees but they may cost different amounts based on the system you use.

## Overview of Polkadot and Ethereum

Let's set the stage by exploring [Polkadot and Ethereum](https://www.soranauts.com/polkadot-vs-ethereum-a-comparative-analysis) – two titans in the blockchain arena with distinct visions of scalability and architecture. Unpacking their differences sheds light on how they update, govern, and ultimately shape the future of decentralized tech.

### Scalability

Scalability is a big deal for blockchains. It means they can handle lots of work without slowing down. Ethereum has had some trouble with this because it gets busy and expensive to use when lots of people are on it at the same time.

Polkadot was designed to be [better at scaling](https://www.soranauts.com/polkadot-parachain-advantages) from the start. It lets many blockchains, called parachains, run side by side. This spreads out the work so no single blockchain gets too crowded.

Substrate helps here too because it makes building these parachains easier. Developers can put together their own custom blockchains that connect back to Polkadot's main network. With this setup, each new chain adds more room for apps and transactions which keeps everything running fast even as more users join in.

### Architectural Differences

Polkadot and Ethereum are built differently. Polkadot uses Substrate, a flexible framework for creating blockchains. This lets developers make custom blockchains with unique features.

On the other hand, Ethereum relies on a more one-size-fits-all model where all dapps run on the same blockchain.

Substrate comes packed with tools like the SRML to help build these customized chains fast. Plus, it lets you use Wasm smart contracts right out of the box! Ethereum's setup is simpler but less customizable.

It has one main blockchain where every dapp shares space and competes for resources. This can lead to traffic jams when it's busy!

### Forks, Upgrades, and Governance

Polkadot and Ethereum both handle changes to their systems in different ways. With Polkadot, upgrades are smooth and don't split the blockchain. This means everyone moves forward together without having to choose between old and new versions.

Their [governance system](https://www.soranauts.com/polkadot-sora-governance-model) lets people who hold DOT tokens help make decisions about how the network should grow.

Ethereum has a different way of making changes called forks. Sometimes these forks can lead to the blockchain splitting into two separate paths when not everyone agrees on an upgrade—a bit like choosing different roads at a crossroad.

Managing these forks is tricky because it's important that all users agree on which path to follow for Ethereum's network to stay strong and work well together.

## Substrate vs EVM: A Comparison

In diving into the realm of blockchain development, we encounter two formidable platforms – Substrate and Ethereum Virtual Machine (EVM) - each with its unique ecosystem. Their battle for supremacy hinges on nuanced differences in abstraction layers and gas fees which, ultimately, shape the developer's journey and the user's experience.

### Layer of Abstraction

Substrate and EVM stand out in blockchain tech because they handle code differently. Think of Substrate like Lego blocks — it gives you the pieces to build a custom chain that can work with Polkadot's ecosystem.

You shape your own rules and how your block connects with others.

Now, EVM is different; it runs on set instructions so Solidity programs can work on any chain using Ethereum's system. This setup lets developers move their projects over without having to change too much stuff.

It’s kind of like plugging a USB drive into any computer – it should just work!

### Gas Fees

Gas fees are like a ticket price for sending transactions or running programs on blockchain networks. These fees keep the network safe by making it costly to do harmful actions, like spamming.

With Substrate and its EVM pallet, a piece of technology in Polkadot's ecosystem, people can build blockchains that talk to Ethereum. This means they could use Etherem's tools but might pay different gas prices.

Think of the [Istanbul hard fork upgrade](https://consensys.io/blog/everything-you-need-to-know-about-the-istanbul-hard-fork) — it made changes to how much gas certain things cost on Ethereum. Substrate EVM aims to follow those same rules for gas costs. So when you do something usual on a substrate-based blockchain that works with Ethereum, you'd pay what you would expect after those updates took effect.

Also, devs are working hard so people can use Solidity — that's Ethereum's programming language — directly on Substrate one day. This could change how much gas you need to spend because it will use a new way called WebAssembly backend.

## Understanding Smart Contracts

Smart contracts stand as the cornerstone of blockchain functionality, revolutionizing how we execute agreements in a trustless environment. Delving into their creation and deployment reveals a stark contrast between Substrate's flexible framework and Ethereum's established virtual machine—each offering distinct pathways for innovation on the decentralized web.

### Difference between developing a smart contract and a parachain

Developing a smart contract is like making an app for a phone. Just as an app works on different phones, a smart contract can work on various blockchains that support them. In Ethereum's world, these contracts are mostly written in Solidity and live on the blockchain, where they run exactly as programmed without any chance of cheating or changing by anybody.

On the other hand, creating a parachain is more like building your own special phone that can do specific things really well. Parachains are custom blockchains that link to Polkadot's main chain, allowing them to communicate with each other and share security.

Substrate makes this possible; it offers tools to build these specialized chains fast and easily. Each [parachain](https://www.soranauts.com/sora-polkadot-parachain-winners) can have its own rules and features while still being part of the larger Polkadot network.

### Building a Smart Contract

Building a smart contract is like crafting a digital agreement. It runs on the blockchain, ensuring rules are followed without needing a middle person.

- Choose the blockchain: Decide if your contract will run on Ethereum or another chain that can handle EVM code.
- Learn Solidity: This is the language you often use for writing smart contracts on Ethereum.
- Set up your environment: You need tools like Truffle or Remix to write and test your contracts.
- Write your code carefully: Your contract must be secure and do exactly what you want it to do. Errors can be costly.
- Test it out: Check your contract in a safe place where you can't lose money. This could be a testnet or simulation.
- Deploy your contract: Put it on the real blockchain when you're sure it works right. Now others can interact with it.
- Verify and publish the source code: This lets others see how your contract works and trust it's fair.

### Frontier EVM Contracts

Frontier EVM contracts let you use Solidity, the programming language for Ethereum, without changing anything. This is great because it means developers can move their work from Ethereum to a Substrate-based chain easily.

Think of it as picking up a house and placing it in a new neighborhood where it fits right in. With these contracts, people building on Polkadot can connect with Ethereum's tools and users.

This makes life simpler for everyone who makes dapps—those are like apps but run on a blockchain. By using Frontier EVM, you get to play with all the Polkadot features while keeping your project open to the big crowd of Ethereum fans.

It's like having a party and inviting friends from two different schools—you get more fun ideas and ways to play!

### Substrate Contracts

Moving from Frontier EVM Contracts to Substrate Contracts brings us into the realm of Polkadot's native smart contract environment. Here, developers use a module known as the Contracts pallet.

This pallet lets coders build and run smart contracts in WebAssembly (Wasm). Unlike EVM, Wasm is designed for high performance on modern web platforms and inside blockchain ecosystems.

With this tool, anyone can make a decentralized app using the Substrate framework.

Substrate's flexibility shines with its support for Wasm. It allows for more efficient code execution than traditional Ethereum smart contracts which rely on EVM bytecode. Plus, thanks to efforts like developing a WebAssembly backend for the Solidity compiler, even existing Solidity projects can transition smoothly onto [Substrate-based blockchains](https://www.soranauts.com/sora-blockchain-new-world-economic-order).

This ensures that switching over or starting fresh on Substrate doesn't mean leaving behind what's already built or mastered in Ethereum's language.

## FAQs

### What's the difference between Substrate and EVM?

Substrate is a framework to build custom blockchains, while EVM, or Ethereum Virtual Machine, runs smart contracts on Ethereum mainnet.

### Can you use Ethereum tools with Substrate?

Absolutely! Substrate comes with an Ethereum compatibility layer so developers can use existing Ethereum tools and ERC-20 tokens on new blockchains.

### How does Polkadot relate to all of this?

Well, Polkadot lets different blockchains built with Substrate work together; it’s all about interoperability!

### Is developing on Substrate similar to using Solidity on EVM?

In some ways—Substrate allows for more flexibility in creating unique runtimes whereas Solidity is a language used strictly for writing smart contracts on EVM-based chains.

### Are my funds safe when transferring from an EVM chain to a Substrate blockchain?

Yes indeed! Transfers keep everything secure through unified accounts and compatibility features that ensure smooth transitions between systems.

### What makes developers choose one over the other—Substrate or EVM chains?

It depends—the choice hinges on what they're building: DeFi apps may fare better with tried-and-tested EVM while cutting-edge projects might leverage Substrates' customizability for something totally unique!

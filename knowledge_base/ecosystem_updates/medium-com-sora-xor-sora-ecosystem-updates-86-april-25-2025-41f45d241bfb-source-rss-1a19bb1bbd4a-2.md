### You Are Here

As the dust settles on the Donald wave — and the economy went along for the ride — the market’s worst players are holed up, licking wounds and counting losses, while crypto’s true believers step back into the spotlight. The clash over whether CBDCs or stablecoins will form the backbone of global financial infrastructure grows ever more heated — picture rival gangs squaring off in a Brazilian favela or a Jamaican shantytown.

Much of the noise comes from the stablecoin camp, chanting mantras of “muh freedom,” “muh decentralization,” and “less government surveillance” — but scratch the surface, and you uncover a fundamental misunderstanding of banking fundamentals and, more alarmingly, the very mechanics that keep these coins “stable.” Take this recent [Polkadot blog post](https://polkadot.com/blog/cbdcs-vs-stablecoins-future-of-digital-currency/) — yes, the network SORA v2 calls home — despite its own [underappreciated CBDC proof of concept](/sora-xor/the-sora-network-hosts-the-first-substrate-polkadot-based-cbdc-in-collaboration-with-the-central-6cc78e9b82b8). It peddles claims like: CBDC programmability will be twisted into limiting how people use their digital monies, that CBDCs risk a single point of failure, and that governments will run the show.

The truth couldn’t be more different. Programmable money caps? That’s a feature no sane central bank would ever deploy — unlike certain stablecoins (_cough, USDT_) that have [frozen user funds](https://tether.io/news/tether-freezes-32-addresses-linked-to-terrorism-and-warfare-in-israel-and-ukraine/) on a whim. Live CBDCs already run on distributed ledgers, so there’s no single node to knock out. And central banks (and muh international finance), not politicians, control the money supply (and hence the politicians, not the other way around); governments _propose_ policy, but they don’t get to print or erase zeroes at will. Even central banks are limited by the realities of the market, and most countries have multiple layers of oversight to make sure no single entity can just do whatever they want.

Sure, you can’t entirely fault what feels like a vibed essay praising stablecoins over CBDCs — after all, much of the real innovation is happening in the Far East, not Silicon Valley or _Bruxelles_. Those markets have bigger fish to fry: geopolitics, trade wars, you name it. Cambodia’s Bakong CBDC has already processed over 330% of the nation’s GDP in 2024 alone; the Solomon Islands are testing CBDCs with public blockchain interoperability; Palau is pioneering novel on-ledger finance; and even the Free Republic of Liberland lets its token roam freely across chains. The takeaway? The SORA wave is starting to crest.

At the end of the day, most folks would rather tap their familiar bank app than chase some newfangled protocol whose greatest claim to fame is a slick logo on a T-shirt. But let’s get back to what actually matters.

While you dig into real-world financial rails and the untapped promise of blockchain (_spoiler: it’s not Ripple_), know that SORA’s latest incarnation — V3 — is forging ahead. The [Fujiwara testnet](/sora-xor/the-fujiwara-testnet-the-next-step-on-the-way-to-sora-v3-c11e49ab496c) is humming along, the Iroha 2 explorer telemetry is 80% done, and we’re eyeing a full release in the next two weeks. On the home front, the new fee model is almost live, priming XOR to become the system’s native medium of exchange. (_This isn’t even my final form._) Stay tuned — more SORA V3 goodies are dropping soon. Join the [Fujiwara Testnet](/sora-xor/the-fujiwara-testnet-the-next-step-on-the-way-to-sora-v3-c11e49ab496c) and see for yourself.

Meanwhile, over on SORA v2-land, SORA’s TON bridge is in the oven, prepping for integration into the Hashi Bridge interface. With so many moving parts, we’re stress-testing every byte to ensure zero leaks. Parallel to that, a landmark partnership with a sovereign nation is kicking off in V3 — no teasers here, but suffice it to say, this will showcase XOR’s power to energize an entire economy and spawn a network effect unlike anything before. As Herr Doktor decreed, “All tokens must be backed by productive work.” And so it shall be.

If you’ve been tracking the network, you caught the [4.6.0 runtime upgrade](https://github.com/sora-xor/sora2-network/releases/tag/4.6.0) on April 10th. It brought the ability for XOR repackaging — so no more wrangling with endless zeroes. Below, you’ll find a deep dive into what this means and what’s next once the community greenlights the new multiplier.

You’re now caught up on the latest developments in the SORA ecosystem and the SORA Integrated Plan. Stay tuned for more updates from our contributors — we’ll be back with more news soon. **Now, let’s get back to work!**

### XOR Repackaging

Because of the large number of XOR and TBDC tokens, to make them more ergonomic, it is proposed to potentially repackage them on the SORA network.

* The supply and amount of XOR and TBCD are being grouped everywhere on the SORA v2 network, where multiple tokens would be grouped into 1 token.

In concrete terms, whatever amount the on-chain Governance agrees on can group multiple XOR into 1 token. For example:.

* The Council agrees to create a motion for denomination.startDenomination(1000)
* If the total supply of XOR is 10,000,000 then after the repackaging, the new number of tokens on the SORA v2 network will be: 10,000,000 / 1,000 = 10,000
* If someone is holding 1,000 XOR, then after the repackaging they will have 1 token worth 1k XOR on the SORA v2 network. If they move it to Ethereum or other networks, it will be unpackaged back to 1,000 XOR tokens. In any case, the total number of XOR in circulation will not change, but the divisible will change on the SORA v2 network if this upgrade happens.

**_(These numbers are for illustrative purposes)_**

This will allow us to continue operations with numbers manageable by the system and users.

### About SORA

[**SORA**](https://sora.org/) is an innovative, adaptive, non-debt-based monetary framework that facilitates economic stability, particularly for financially vulnerable countries. As a groundbreaking platform, SORA exemplifies the potential of blockchain technology in fostering a more inclusive and stable global financial system. Its integration in projects like the Bokolo Cash CBDC signifies a new era in digital currency, aligning technological advancements to achieve broader economic stability and resilience. Through its unique approach and capabilities, SORA plays a pivotal role in the evolution of global finance, particularly by supporting economies that need innovative and sustainable financial solutions the most.

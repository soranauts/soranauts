# Macroscopic Structure of the Supply-Chain Network and the Growth of Firms in Japan

## Abstract

We construct the Japanese production network at the firm-level, using the dataset of financial statements and supplier-customer relationships for one million firms in Japan collected by Tokyo Shoko Research Inc. from 2011 to 2016. Recent research reported that the Japanese production network has a tightly-knot structure with a core of giant strongly connected component (GSCC) surrounded by its upstream (IN) and downstream (OUT) components constituting two half-shells. In this paper, we analyze its macroscopic structure and evolution. Larger firms obeying a power-law in size distribution follow Pareto?s and Gibrat?s law, where has the variance of their growth rate does not depend on size. Therefore, we focus on the relation between the macroscopic structure and firms? size and its growth rate measured by sales. Major results of this study are as follows: First, the firms obeying Pareto?s law are mainly composed of GSCC, and the firms belonging to IN tend to be smaller than the firms in the other components. Second, although about half of supplier-customer links are rewired in five years, the Japanese production network has stable firms not moving between components of macroscopic structure. Third, we can observe Gibrat?s law for such stable firms for each component, but the applicable region of firms located IN component is extremely smaller than the other components. The findings suggest that the hierarchy and stability in the production network help characterize the stability of firm growth in Japan.

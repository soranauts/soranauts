# Multilayer Network Analysis for Industrial Supply Chains of Japanese Prefecture

## Abstract

Understanding the industrial structure of inner and inter prefectures is crucial for policymakers to make economic policies according to evidence. To address this issue, using the dataset of financial statements and connections for one million firms in Japan collected by Tokyo Shoko Research Inc., we construct a multiplex supply network with 47 layers equivalent to prefectures. We investigate the prefectural economic circulation in terms of the bow-tie structure of the prefectural networks at the firm level. Applying clustering analysis based on the Jensen?Shannon distance between networks and the community detection techniques known as the Infomap method to this multiplex supply network, we clarify industrial structural similarities and differences for each prefecture. Our findings of this study are as follows. First, since prefectures near large metropolitan areas form an economic circulation centered on the central prefectures such as Tokyo and Osaka, these prefectures near them such as Chiba, Saitama, and Nara have extremely low fractions of GSCC, and about 40% of firms in those prefectures belong to DIS components. Second, from 2011 to 2018, the industrial networks of 47 prefectures can be classified into three structural patterns by a degree of urbanization. Third, the hierarchical community structure can be observed using firm-level data. Our findings suggest a new classification approach for prefectures based on similarities in the industrial structure and contribute to a better insight into the geographical characteristics of each region?s industrial structure.

# The Case for XOR

## Abstract

XOR is a supranational currency whose supply and primary market are managed by a computer program that runs on a blockchain.  
It is not controlled by any country, company, or individual — instead, it forms a global economic system designed for the common good of humanity.

To facilitate economic growth, money must be deployed productively to create new goods and services.  
Unlike other cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin, countries can make proposals to fund productive investments via XOR allocated from a fund, or through the minting of TBCD (Token Bonding Curve Dollar), which is convertible to XOR on the open market.  
All of this occurs via an open governance process.  
This means XOR can integrate into domestic economies to fund nominal GDP growth, rather than acting as an external foreign currency.  

Because XOR is not debt-based, more egalitarian social outcomes can be achieved — economic growth is not inhibited by the need to service compounding debt.  
When used as legal tender for debts, purchases, and taxes, XOR can drive economic expansion by directly funding productive output.

## 1. Introduction

Money allows finite resources to be allocated efficiently without central planning, which is computationally impossible to optimize in real time.  
It provides a “satisficing” (good-enough) solution to resource allocation and can uplift societies if economic systems are rationally constructed for socially beneficial outcomes.

Money has three defining properties:
1. **Unit of account** — measure of value  
2. **Store of value**  
3. **Medium of exchange**

These functions solve the problem of trust in exchange and resource allocation.  
Historically, both privately issued and central-bank-issued money have succeeded and failed under different circumstances.

With the rise of cryptocurrencies, new debates have emerged about non-central-bank digital money in 21st-century economies.  
Cryptocurrencies are cryptographically secured assets transacted on distributed ledger technologies (DLTs) such as blockchains.  
Their monetary utility depends on how well they fulfill money’s three functions.

Compared to debt-based fiat systems, cryptocurrencies can offer advantages in:
- cross-border interoperability  
- speed and efficiency  
- security and transparency  
- decentralization and resilience  
- programmability and independence  

They are natively digital and suitable for complex modern economies, including automated and machine-to-machine transactions.

This paper explores how XOR can help countries achieve predictable economic growth.  
It first defines economic growth, then explains how XOR can promote it, compares XOR to Bitcoin as legal tender, and outlines steps for national adoption.

## 2. Creating Economic Growth

Economic growth is an increase in transactions contributing to GDP — achieved by expanding or improving the use of factor inputs: **land, labor, capital, and technology**.

The potential output of a country can be written as:

> **Y\*** = f(QFI\*, TFP\*)  
> where QFI\* = potential quantity of factor inputs, and  
> TFP\* = potential total factor productivity.

To reach potential output, inputs must be used optimally to maximize value creation.

The **equation of exchange** describes how money supply relates to economic activity:

> M × V = P × Q

where:  
M = money supply,  
V = velocity of money,  
P = price level,  
Q = quantity of goods/services sold.

When measured via GDP:

> M × V = P × Y

Richard Werner and others have shown that the equation’s usefulness depends on distinguishing how money is used:

- **Mᴿ** = money used for real goods/services (contributes to GDP)  
- **Mᶠ** = money used for asset speculation (not counted in GDP)

Thus:

> ΔMᴿ × Vᴿ = Δ(Pᴿ × Yᴿ)  
> ΔMᶠ × Vᶠ = Δ(Pᶠ × Qᶠ)

These show that **how money is created and allocated** determines:
1. Economic growth or contraction  
2. Price stability  
3. Asset bubbles

Because Vᴿ (real-sector velocity) and Pᴿ (prices) are stable, increasing Mᴿ (productive money) directly increases GDP.

### Interest Rates Misconception

Interest rates are **not** causal for GDP growth.  
They are the price of borrowing, not a determinant of the money supply.  
Empirical data show **nominal GDP growth causes higher rates**, not vice versa.

### FDI Misconception

Foreign direct investment (FDI) rarely produces sustainable growth when the foreign currency used is outside domestic control.  
Competent investors extract more value than they create.  
Hence, national monetary policy should focus on **domestic money creation for productive output**, not foreign capital inflows.

**Summary:**  
Economic growth arises when money is created and allocated to produce new goods and services that expand real output.

## 3. XOR as a Catalyst for Economic Growth

XOR is designed to overcome volatility, governance gaps, and inefficiency found in early cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin.  
It is the **native token of the SORA decentralized economic system**, created to fund productive output of goods and services.

The model draws on empirical research by Fisher, Werner, Shimomura, Kurihara, and Yamaguchi, and on SORAMITSU founder Makoto Takemiya’s prior work.

To enable growth, money should:
1. Be issued in response to real economic demand.  
2. Resist arbitrary issuance (political misuse).  
3. Provide forward guidance on liquidity and price.

### Token Bonding Curve (TBC)

XOR achieves this via a **token bonding curve**, a transparent algorithm (smart contract) that elastically adjusts supply.

- **Buy-price function:** starts at **\$634 per XOR**, increasing by \$1 for every 1,337 XOR issued.  
  Buying mints new XOR and deposits the payment currency into reserves.  
- **Sell-price function:** buys back XOR at **20% less** than the buy-price function, burning XOR in return for reserves.  
  When reserves are low, it continues along a continuous curve.

Because the TBC mints and burns XOR dynamically, supply scales to any economy size, ensuring predictable purchasing power.

### Technical Efficiency

- Built on **Parity Substrate** (now evolving toward Hyperledger Iroha v3 in SORA v3).  
- Transaction time ≈ 3 seconds, cost ≈ a few cents.  
- No mining or energy waste — extremely power-efficient.  
- Proven scalability: 100 million accounts, 6 000 TPS.  
- Deployed successfully on **Polkaswap** (since 2021) with millions of secure transactions.

### Governance and Allocation

The XOR economy is governed democratically via **on-chain referenda**.  
Token holders vote on upgrades, treasury allocations, and TBCD issuance.

Governments can propose productive funding initiatives (e.g., R&D, infrastructure) through TBCD minting — convertible to XOR via the TBC.  
This aligns incentives because bureaucrats’ and citizens’ holdings are affected equally by inflationary effects, promoting careful, value-creating proposals.

XOR eliminates the need for taxation to fund public goods:  
governments are financed through **democratically allocated XOR**, not extraction from producers.  
This structure:
- Removes moral hazard,  
- Discourages corruption,  
- Realigns incentives toward productive output.

Overall, XOR enables **self-governing, productive economies** without debt or excessive taxation.

## 4. Comparison to Bitcoin Legal Tender Status in El Salvador

El Salvador’s 2021 Bitcoin Law recognized Bitcoin as legal tender for debts, payments, and taxes — aiming to lower remittance costs, bank the unbanked, and reduce USD dependency.

Bitcoin distribution relies on the **Chivo Wallet** app (built on Algorand for faster transactions) and physical ATMs.  
Early metrics showed high adoption — 3 million users (≈ 50% of the population) due in part to a \$30 sign-up bonus — but long-term growth remains uncertain.

### Short-Term Results
- More inflow than outflow at Bitcoin ATMs  
- Fuel discounts for Bitcoin users (via Puma Energy & Uno Terra)  
- A \$150 M Bitcoin Trust gained \$5 M in surplus for public spending (e.g., a veterinary hospital)

### International Response
Institutions like the IMF and World Bank criticized the move, citing volatility and dollarization risks.  
Regional banks (e.g., CABEI) offered technical assistance.

### Structural Limitations
- **Bitcoin acts as a foreign currency.**  
  New BTC cannot be minted domestically to fund production.  
  Growth depends on inflows from abroad, mirroring dollarized constraints.  
- **Fixed supply (21 million)** makes it deflationary and unsuited for continuous economic expansion.  
- **Proof-of-Work** consumes massive energy with no direct productive output.  
- When block rewards decline, network security may falter unless fees skyrocket or rules change.

### Why XOR Differs
- XOR is not foreign; governments can mint or request XOR via governance to fund production directly.  
- Money supply expands only for **productive uses**, preserving price stability.  
- Energy-efficient, fast, and democratic — no mining, no external dependency.  

In essence, **XOR functions as adaptive domestic money within a supranational system**, enabling growth without foreign control or energy waste.

## 5. Steps to Implement the SORA Economic System in a Country

To adopt XOR as legal tender:

1. **Legislate legal tender status**  
   XOR must be valid for:
   - debt repayment  
   - goods and services  
   - tax and government fees  
   Countries may choose:
   - **Option 1:** exclusive XOR currency  
   - **Option 2:** parallel use with existing fiat (as El Salvador did)

2. **Deploy localized SORA Wallet**  
   Customize the open-source SORA mobile wallet with national branding and language.  
   Funding is available through the **SORA Builders Programme** for software localization and integrations.

3. **Appoint government liaisons**  
   Assign a coordinator (or committee) to communicate with the SORA ecosystem, draft proposals, and analyze factor-input utilization.  
   The SORA community can assist in setting up this process.

4. **Rollout and economic coordination**  
   Governments can begin using XOR immediately upon legal approval, at virtually no cost.  
   In parallel-currency mode, systemic risk is minimal — markets will naturally choose the most efficient currency.

## 6. Conclusions

Creating money for **new goods and services** drives growth without consumer inflation.  
Monetary creation and allocation are society’s most powerful tools for prosperity.

Cryptocurrencies make global trade more efficient, but legacy systems like Bitcoin cannot create new money domestically and thus limit growth potential.

XOR, however:
- Removes technical and economic limitations,  
- Supports millions of users efficiently,  
- Enables governments to allocate new XOR for productive uses.

By adopting XOR as legal tender, nations can join a **supranational, decentralized economic system** that promotes open trade and equitable growth — without debt, exploitation, or foreign dependence.
